Family explanation

The North Glen emblem

The emblem records the identity of North Glen under the Lewis family, the current owners. Its colours and symbols were chosen to represent the land, farming life and Australia.

The lower scroll carries the motto OPERARII TERRAE DEI, meaning “Workers of God’s Land.”

Meaning of the colours and symbols

Deep blue

The blue represents the water and dams on North Glen.

Golden yellow

The yellow represents the golden fields of North Glen.

The oak tree

The tree represents the original oak on the farm, preserving an important feature of North Glen in the emblem.

The farm animals

The animals represent the livestock and animals of North Glen and the property’s farming identity.

The Southern Cross

The Southern Cross represents Australia and North Glen’s place within the Australian landscape.

The motto

OPERARII TERRAE DEI means “Workers of God’s Land”, expressing the family’s view of working and caring for the land.

Lewis family heraldry

The Lewis family are the current owners of North Glen. This section records the family meaning behind the North Glen heraldry rather than treating the symbols as an unresolved historical puzzle. The emblem brings together the water, dams, golden fields, original oak, farm animals, the Southern Cross and the motto “Workers of God’s Land.”

Earlier family history

The Mathewson family belongs to the earlier family history being researched for North Glen. Their connection should be documented separately from the present-day Lewis family heraldry so that the historic ownership story and the meaning of the current emblem are not confused.

What counts as heraldry?

A shield-shaped property logo can use heraldic visual language without necessarily being an officially granted coat of arms. Before describing the North Glen emblem as historical family arms, the archive would need evidence showing its designer, date, ownership and whether it was formally granted, inherited or simply created as a modern property emblem.

Other identity evidence

For a farm, historical identity may survive not only in shields and logos but also in wool-bale stencils, livestock brands, ear marks, gate signs, stationery, invoices, sale catalogues, letterheads, engraved silver, photographs and newspaper advertisements. Finding any of these for North Glen could be just as historically important as the modern emblem.
